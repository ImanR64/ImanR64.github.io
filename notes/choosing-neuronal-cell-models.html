SH-SY5Y, PC12 or iPSC-derived neurons? Choosing a cell model for synaptic studies

A cell that looks like a neuron is not necessarily a neuron that makes working synapses. What our recent studies show, and how to choose the right model for your question.

By Dr. Iman Rostami · 25 November 2026 · 5 min read

Neuronal cell lines are cheap, fast and easy to scale, so they appear in a large share of neuroscience papers. But neuronal markers and long neurites do not prove that a cell forms functional synapses. Over the past years, my colleagues and I tested this directly, combining electron microscopy, cryo-electron tomography and electrophysiology. Here is what we learned, and how to choose.

PC12 cells: neuron-like, but synaptogenesis stays incomplete

After treatment with nerve growth factor (NGF), rat PC12 cells grow neurites, become electrically excitable and cluster presynaptic proteins such as synaptophysin and Rab3a. Under the electron microscope they even show presynaptic-like structures. Yet they lack the fully developed presynaptic organisation needed for sustained synaptic vesicle recycling [1].

Good forNeurite outgrowth, neurosecretion, early differentiation and toxicity screens
Not forQuestions about mature synaptic transmission

SH-SY5Y cells: neuronal markers, immature synapses

The human neuroblastoma line SH-SY5Y is usually differentiated with retinoic acid and BDNF. The cells express classic neuronal markers and look convincingly neuronal. When we analysed several differentiation protocols structurally and functionally, the cells still lacked synaptic maturity [2].

Good forHuman-specific protein biology, high-throughput screens, neurite and marker studies
Not forConclusions about functional synapses, unless you show synapse formation yourself

iPSC-derived neurons: closest to the real thing, with effort

Human iPSC-derived neurons, including motor neurons, carry a patient's own genetic background and can form functional synapses. They are slower, more expensive and more variable. To improve synapse formation, we added two glutamatergic modulators during motor-neuron differentiation: CX516, a positive modulator of AMPA receptors, and CDPPB, a positive modulator of mGluR5. Within 28 days the neurons formed mature networks, and the modulators increased synaptic density and improved electrophysiological and ultrastructural measures of synaptic connectivity. The resulting synapses can also be imaged in 3D by cryo-electron tomography [3].

Good forDisease modelling (for example ALS), patient-specific questions, synaptic physiology and structure
Plan forLonger timelines, higher cost and careful batch-to-batch controls

A quick decision guide

Your questionReasonable first model
Neurite growth, toxicity screen, many conditionsPC12 or SH-SY5Y
Human protein biology, no synapses neededSH-SY5Y
Synaptic vesicle release, synaptic transmissioniPSC-derived or primary neurons
Patient mutation, disease mechanismiPSC-derived neurons from patients, with isogenic controls

Whichever model you choose, check synapse formation directly, by electron microscopy, co-localisation of pre- and postsynaptic markers, or electrophysiology, instead of inferring it from neuronal markers.
